## Victoria and BC Modernism

**Segger, Martin. *Conservation Guidelines for Modernist Architecture in the Victoria Region.* 2019, 55 pp.** Commissioned by the Victoria Civic Heritage Trust, Victoria Heritage Foundation, Saanich Heritage Foundation, and Oak Bay Heritage Foundation. Defines the period 1935–1975 for Victoria's modernist architectural heritage and provides practical conservation strategies. The single most important published source for this project. Available at [UVic DSpace](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/handle/1828/11482) and [Heritage BC](https://heritagebc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Segger_Martin_ConservGuide_2019.pdf).

**Segger, Martin. "Victoria Modern." UVic Legacy Art Galleries, 2012–2017.** A series of exhibitions and publications exploring the personalities, relationships, and projects behind Victoria's post-war modernist architecture (1939–2013). Features detailed case studies of architects including John Di Castri and John Wade and Partners. The project's scope itself demonstrates that only selected landmark buildings had been systematically documented. [legacy.uvic.ca](https://legacy.uvic.ca/gallery/victoriamodern/)

**Segger, Martin, and Douglas Franklin. *Victoria: A Primer for Regional History in Architecture 1843–1929.* Heritage Architectural Guides, 1979.** The foundational survey of Victoria's built heritage. Covers only up to 1929, leaving the entire modernist period without a comparable architectural history — a gap that remains largely unfilled.

**UVic Libraries. "Mid-Century Architecture in Victoria." Digital exhibit.** Defines the Canadian mid-century modern period as "roughly the end of World War II in 1945 to the Canadian Centennial in 1967," with a stricter definition of a "long 1950s." Documents three featured architects: John Wade and Partners, John Di Castri, and Hubert Norbury. [exhibits.library.uvic.ca](https://exhibits.library.uvic.ca/spotlight/mcm/feature/1-introduction)

**Windsor-Liscombe, Rhodri. *The New Spirit: Modern Architecture in Vancouver, 1938–1963.* Canadian Centre for Architecture / MIT Press, 1997.** The first comprehensive study of Vancouver modernism. Covers the broader BC context relevant to Victoria's post-war architectural development.

**Kalman, Harold. *A History of Canadian Architecture.* Oxford University Press, 1994, 2 vols.** Volume 2 covers modernism and notes Vancouver as "Canada's prototypically modern city." A *Concise History* edition was published in 2000.

## Modern Heritage in Canada

**Algie, Susan, and James Ashby, eds. *Conserving the Modern in Canada: Buildings, Ensembles, and Sites, 1945–2005.* Winnipeg Architecture Foundation / DOCOMOMO Canada-Ontario, 2007.** Proceedings of Canada's first national conference on modern heritage (Trent University, Peterborough, May 2005). Defines the period of focus as 1945–1975 for the Canadian context. Available at [winnipegarchitecture.ca](http://winnipegarchitecture.ca/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/CMC-Proceedings_Eng_a.pdf).

**Jonas Galvin, Kayla. "Mad About Modernism." *Municipal World*, September 2014 (published March 2017).** Identifies public apathy as the greatest risk to mid-century modern buildings in Canada. Notes the existence of an active DOCOMOMO BC chapter and the listing of Vancouver's Main Post Office (1953–1959) on the 2013 Endangered Places list. [municipalworld.com](https://www.municipalworld.com/feature-story/mad-about-modernism/)

**National Trust for Canada. Endangered Places List (annual, since 2003).** Has featured multiple modernist buildings, several of which were subsequently demolished. [nationaltrustcanada.ca](https://nationaltrustcanada.ca/what-we-offer/endangered-places)

**Parks Canada. *Standards and Guidelines for the Conservation of Historic Places in Canada.* 2nd ed., 2011.** The national standard for heritage conservation. The second edition explicitly addresses modern materials and assemblies. [historicplaces.ca](https://www.historicplaces.ca/en/pages/standards-normes.aspx)

## Heritage Protection and Policy

**City of Victoria. Heritage Conservation.** The City's Heritage Register lists properties with heritage value, but "by itself, listing a property on the Heritage Register does not restrict any future actions proposed by an owner." Only heritage-*designated* properties are protected from alteration or demolition without Council approval. [victoria.ca](https://www.victoria.ca/building-business/permits-development-construction/heritage-conservation)

**Policy Options (IRPP). "Canada has occasional policy but no effective statutes to protect heritage structures." August 2021.** Argues that Canada "actually incentivizes destruction of built-heritage assets through a tax system biased toward demolition." [policyoptions.irpp.org](https://policyoptions.irpp.org/magazines/august-2021/canada-has-occasional-policy-but-no-effective-statutes-to-protect-heritage-structures/)

**Focus on Victoria. "Demolishing the Past, Unbuilding the Future."** Reports that demolitions in Victoria peaked in 2015 at 53 houses, with approximately 375 houses demolished in the City of Victoria over the preceding decade. [focusonvictoria.ca](https://www.focusonvictoria.ca/reporting/29/)

## Citizen Science and Heritage Documentation

**Orr, Scott A., Lyn Wilson, et al. "Using Citizen Heritage Science to Monitor Remote Sites Before and During the First COVID-19 Lockdown: A Comparison of Two Methods." *Conservation and Management of Archaeological Sites* 24:4–6 (2022).** Demonstrates that community volunteers with smartphones can gather reliable heritage documentation data, overcoming the capacity limitations of institutional organizations. [doi.org](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13505033.2022.2147299)

**Bai, Nan, et al. "Wiki Loves Monuments: Crowdsourcing the Collective Image of the Worldwide Built Heritage." *Journal on Computing and Cultural Heritage* (ACM, 2022).** Analyses Wiki Loves Monuments, the world's largest photography competition, which has collected approximately 2 million photographs of built heritage across 90 countries through volunteer participation. [doi.org](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3569092)

**CitizenHeritage. EU-funded project on citizen science practices in cultural heritage.** Identified 110 international cases of citizen participation in heritage documentation. [citizenheritage.eu](https://www.citizenheritage.eu/)

**British Library. *The Collective Wisdom Handbook: Perspectives on Crowdsourcing in Cultural Heritage.*** Surveys the field of crowdsourcing in cultural heritage institutions. [britishlibrary.pubpub.org](https://britishlibrary.pubpub.org/pub/what-is-crowdsourcing-in-cultural-heritage)

## Local Heritage Organizations

**Hallmark Heritage Society (Victoria, est. 1973).** Maintains archives on over 3,500 buildings in Greater Victoria, much of it compiled through volunteer effort — a longstanding local example of community-based heritage documentation. [hallmarkheritagesociety.ca](https://hallmarkheritagesociety.ca/)

**Victoria Heritage Foundation.** Co-funded Segger's 2019 conservation guidelines. Publishes walking tour guides and maintains resources on Victoria's architectural heritage. [victoriaheritagefoundation.ca](https://victoriaheritagefoundation.ca/publications-resources/)

**Vancouver Heritage Foundation.** Runs annual Mid-Century Modern House Tours celebrating architects from the 1940s–1970s. [vancouverheritagefoundation.org](https://www.vancouverheritagefoundation.org/resources/vhf-archive/past-house-tour-resources/mid-century-modern-house-tour/)

## International Framework

**DOCOMOMO (Documentation and Conservation of buildings, sites, and neighbourhoods of the Modern Movement).** International organization with a BC chapter. Co-organized the *Conserving the Modern in Canada* conference. Provides style definitions and evaluation frameworks for modernist architecture. [docomomo-us.org](https://www.docomomo-us.org/explore-modern/styles-of-the-modern-era)

**UNESCO / ICOMOS / DOCOMOMO Joint Programme on Modern Heritage.** The international framework for identification and documentation of modern heritage. [whc.unesco.org](https://whc.unesco.org/en/modernheritage/)
