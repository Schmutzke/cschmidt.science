## Where I come from

I was born in the 1980s in a country that no longer exists — the German Democratic Republic. I grew up in a system where knowledge was wielded as a means of exercising control. Books, magazines, school curricula — all were instruments of ideology, carefully curated to shape how people understood the world. The state decided what was worth knowing.

But there was resilience. And it was rooted in stories.

I come from a blue-collar family of farmers, masons, and carpenters — hard workers, but avid readers and storytellers. On my father's side were the readers. He and my grandmother kept shelves full of books — uncommon for a farming family — that told different stories. They understood these to be more than a family treasure and shared them widely with friends, neighbours, and community.

On my mother's side were the storytellers. She and my grandfather had a gift for keeping memories alive while making room for imagination, vision, and hope.

The private libraries at home and the oral histories passed across kitchen tables were my first encounters with knowledge that challenged dominant narratives. They taught me, long before I had the language for it, that access to information is never just a technical question. It is a political one — and one of identity.

When the Berlin Wall came down, I became the first person in my family to attend university and trained as a librarian. Throughout my journey I came to understand that libraries are not to be defined as 'places that hold books'. They are institutions built on a conviction: that most knowledge belongs to everyone, unconditionally. And that this conviction has the power to hold democratic societies together.

Everything I have done since follows from that understanding.

## What I believe

**Knowledge is a common good.** Libraries are among the most powerful institutions we have for making that belief real — spaces where access to information is not a privilege but a right, regardless of who you are or where you come from.

My work spans open scholarship, AI literacy, and digital innovation in libraries. What connects it all is a commitment to removing barriers — whether they are paywalls, technological divides, or social exclusion — so that knowledge can serve everyone.

## What I work on

### Open Scholarship & Access

Breaking down barriers to research and information. I have led open access transformation initiatives, managed acquisitions consortia, and published on how libraries can resist predatory publishing and fight misinformation. Open access is not just a policy — it is a question of justice.

→ [[Publications]]

### AI Literacy & Education

Emerging technologies should empower, not exclude. I teach AI literacy, prompt engineering, and citizen science skills at two German universities, and I co-chair a Community of Practice on AI application for librarians at UVic. My goal is to make sure that understanding AI is not reserved for technologists but available to researchers, students, and the public.

→ [[Teaching]]

### Libraries as Public Infrastructure

Libraries are more than collections — they are the infrastructure of an informed society. My research on homeless people as library patrons examined what it means to truly keep knowledge unconditionally accessible. At Kula: Library Futures Academy, I work on building the future of libraries as inclusive public institutions.

→ [[Projects]]

### Digital Preservation & Innovation

Knowledge only remains a common good if it endures. I teach digitalization and long-term preservation, and my work in digital library innovation focuses on ensuring that today's scholarship remains accessible for future generations.
