The [Greater Victoria Modernist Building Inventory](Greater%20Victoria%20Modernist%20Building%20Inventory) asks citizens to identify mid-century modernist features from the street. The checklist is grounded in established architectural preservation literature, adapted for non-expert observers.

## Feature Checklist

| Feature | Description | Primary source |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Flat roof | A roof with no visible slope | Le Corbusier (1927); DOCOMOMO |
| Unusual roof shape | Butterfly, V-shape, folded-plate, or dramatically angled roof | DOCOMOMO US styles guide |
| Ribbon windows | Long, continuous horizontal window bands | Le Corbusier (1927) |
| Extensive glazing | Large glass walls or floor-to-ceiling windows | DOCOMOMO International Style definition |
| Pilotis | Columns lifting the building off the ground | Le Corbusier (1927) |
| Brise soleil | Fins, louvers, or projecting slats for sun shading | Le Corbusier; DOCOMOMO |
| Decorative screen | Perforated concrete or metal screens (breeze blocks) | SurveyLA; DOCOMOMO |
| Exposed concrete | Raw concrete surfaces as a deliberate design choice | DOCOMOMO Brutalist definition |
| Deep overhangs | Deep roof overhangs, cantilevered canopies, or projecting upper floors | NPS Preservation Brief 17 |
| Expressed structure | Visible posts and beams as exterior design elements | DOCOMOMO; Eichler Homes documentation |

## Sources

### Foundational framework

**Le Corbusier, "Five Points of a New Architecture" (1927).** The theoretical origin of several features on our checklist: pilotis, ribbon windows, free facade (extensive glazing), and flat roof (roof garden). Le Corbusier's five points became the canonical definition of modernist architectural principles and remain the starting point for any identification of modernist features. See [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22Five_Points_of_a_New_Architecture%22).

### Survey methodology

**US National Park Service, Preservation Brief 17: "Architectural Character — Identifying the Visual Aspects of Historic Buildings."** The standard methodology for visual building surveys, organised around observable categories: overall shape, roof and roof features, projections, recesses, openings (windows and doors), and exterior materials. Not specific to modernism, but it provides the framework for what non-experts can reliably identify from the street. Available at [nps.gov](https://www.nps.gov/tps/how-to-preserve/briefs/17-architectural-character.htm).

### Modernist style definitions

**DOCOMOMO US, "Styles of the Modern Era."** DOCOMOMO (Documentation and Conservation of buildings, sites, and neighbourhoods of the Modern Movement) provides character-defining feature lists for each modernist sub-style: International Style, Brutalist, Mid-Century Modern, New Formalist, Googie, and others. The International Style entry defines the absence of ornament, flat roofs, smooth uniform wall surfaces, ribbon windows, cantilevers, and corner-turning windows. Available at [docomomo-us.org](https://www.docomomo-us.org/explore-modern/styles-of-the-modern-era).

**DOCOMOMO US, "How to Evaluate Modern."** Provides the evaluation framework for modern-era buildings, covering technological, social, artistic/aesthetic, canonic, and referential merit. Defines the modern period as roughly 1930s–1980s. Available at [docomomo-us.org](https://docomomo-us.org/explore-modern/explore-the-register/how-to-evaluate-modern).

### Canadian context

**Parks Canada, *Standards and Guidelines for the Conservation of Historic Places in Canada*, 2nd ed. (2011).** The Canadian national standard for heritage conservation. The second edition explicitly addresses modern materials and assemblies. Uses a values-based approach to identifying tangible features that express heritage significance. Available at [historicplaces.ca](https://www.historicplaces.ca/en/pages/standards-normes.aspx).

### Field survey precedent

**SurveyLA / Los Angeles Citywide Historic Context Statement, "L.A. Modernism, 1919–1980."** The largest modern-era architectural field survey ever conducted, covering 880,000 parcels. Includes character-defining feature lists for each modernist sub-style and demonstrates how citizen-accessible categories can be applied at scale. Referenced via [DOCOMOMO US Register](https://docomomo-us.org/register/surveyla).

### International framework

**UNESCO / ICOMOS / DOCOMOMO Joint Programme on Modern Heritage.** The international framework for identification and documentation of modern heritage, establishing that modern-era buildings require dedicated survey approaches distinct from traditional heritage methods. See [UNESCO World Heritage Centre](https://whc.unesco.org/en/modernheritage/).

## Design decisions

**Why "extensive glazing" instead of "curtain wall"?** A curtain wall is defined by its non-structural relationship to the building frame — something invisible from the street. Citizens cannot tell whether a glass facade is load-bearing. "Extensive glazing" captures the same visual observation without requiring structural knowledge.

**Why separate "flat roof" and "unusual roof shape"?** Roof form is the single most visible identifier of modernist buildings (NPS Brief 17 lists it as the first category to assess). Flat roofs are the most common modernist type, but butterfly roofs, folded-plate roofs, and dramatic angular forms are equally distinctive and should not be collapsed into a single category.

**Why include "decorative screen"?** Perforated concrete screens (breeze blocks) are among the most visually distinctive and easily recognised mid-century features, yet they are absent from Le Corbusier's five points because they became widespread only in the 1950s–1960s. SurveyLA and DOCOMOMO both identify them as character-defining.
