## AI Literacy

Emerging technologies should empower, not exclude. I teach AI Literacy and Prompt Engineering at a German university. My goal is to make sure that understanding AI is not reserved for technologists but available to researchers, students, and the public. At UVic, I helped shape the library's early response to generative AI — documented in [UVic Libraries, AI & Student Success](http://hdl.handle.net/1828/15694).

## Digital Preservation

Knowledge only remains a common good if it endures. I teach digitalization and long-term preservation at [Leipzig University of Applied Sciences (HTWK Leipzig)](https://www.htwk-leipzig.de/en/studieren/studiengaenge/masterstudiengaenge/bibliotheks-und-informationswissenschaft-master), ensuring that today's scholarship remains accessible for future generations.

## Future of Libraries

For as long as I have been a professional in the field of knowledge preservation and organization, I have been fascinated by the question of what tomorrow's libraries will look like. At [Kula: Library Futures Academy](https://www.uvic.ca/library/about-us/kula-academy/index.php), I am part of a team that works on building that future. A playful entry point into this question is my 2015 contribution to [*Zeitreisen in die bibliothekarische Zukunft*](https://portal.dnb.de/opac.htm?method=simpleSearch&cqlMode=true&query=idn%3D1065135726) — a futurology imagining libraries in 2114 (German, print only).

## Open Scholarship

Open scholarship is not just a policy — it is a question of equity and inclusion. In my work, I have managed acquisitions & collections of a university library with the purpose of breaking down barriers to information. I have co-chaired a [state-wide library consortium](Service) tasked to steward the digital turn through its funding. I have overseen the operations of a university press and its strategic development towards an open access publisher. And in my role as an instructor across university programs, I advocate for open practices in learning and teaching.

## Professional Ethics

Librarianship is built on strong values — access, inclusion, intellectual freedom. But how well does the profession live up to them in practice? My research keeps asking that question. 

I have examined library engagement with the [UN Sustainable Development Goals](https://sdgs.un.org/goals) and whether the profession's commitment to social inclusion holds up in practice. My chapter on [homeless people as library patrons](https://doi.org/10.18452/20199) (German) confronts the gap between professional ideals and institutional reality. 

My master's thesis investigated how librarians navigate the tensions between professional ethics and the realities of academic self-publishing. 

This critical lens drives my work: not to undermine the profession, but to strengthen it.

## Scholarly Communication

My research examines how scholars publish and how libraries can help them navigate an increasingly complex publishing landscape. My master's thesis analyzed how the library profession engages with [academic self-publishing](https://doi.org/10.18452/27508) (Abstract in English,  In German) — revealing tensions between professional ethics and market realities. I have also published on how libraries can help scholars [resist predatory publishing](https://nbn-resolving.org/urn:nbn:de:0290-opus4-168032) (German).

## Truth, Knowledge & Society

Libraries are more than collections — they are the infrastructure of an informed society. My work explores how libraries serve diverse publics: from open knowledge platforms to feminist libraries, from services for the visually impaired to unconditional access for homeless populations. These are questions I have explored long before joining [Kula](https://www.uvic.ca/library/about-us/kula-academy/index.php) — and that now define my work there. An early example is my investigation into how libraries can help scholars and the public [identify and resist predatory publishing](https://nbn-resolving.org/urn:nbn:de:0290-opus4-168032) (German).
